How to Host a Murder Mystery Dinner Party at Home

no acting experience required

A good murder mystery dinner party runs on three things: a little advance planning, guests who commit to their character for one evening, and a host willing to play detective-slash-narrator. Here's how to pull it off without it feeling like homework for anyone.

1. Pick a mystery that fits your table

Match the mystery to your headcount and the vibe you're going for. A 10-player dinner party mystery like XOXO, You're Dead works well for a full table and a few hours of playtime; smaller gatherings do better with a 4–6 player format so nobody's character feels like an afterthought.

2. Send character assignments early

Give guests their character dossier a few days ahead so they can read their backstory, secret, and motive before they arrive. It's the difference between people playing a character and people just reading their card out loud at the table.

3. Set the table, then let the evidence do the work

You don't need a full costume budget — name tags, a printed seating chart, and revealing evidence cards in rounds is enough to keep the night moving. Space the evidence reveals between courses so the mystery unfolds alongside dinner instead of all at once.

4. Save the reveal for last

Every kit includes a host-only solution with a full confession monologue — read it after the final verdict ballot is in, not before. Half the fun is guests realizing how close (or far off) their guesses were.
