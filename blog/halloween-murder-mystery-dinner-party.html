How to Throw a Halloween Murder Mystery Dinner Party

a whodunit for a small, spooky town

A Halloween murder mystery swaps the usual candy-and-costumes routine for something guests actually have to work for: a case to solve, a character to play, and a killer sitting at the table with everyone else. Here's how to run one without it turning into chaos by course two.

1. Pick a mystery built for a crowd

A Halloween party often means a bigger guest list than your average dinner party, so look for a mystery built to scale. Haunted Hollow is written for up to 15 players, with a small-town setting that gives every guest a built-in reason to know (and suspect) everyone else — neighbors, shopkeepers, the town's own secrets. Bigger casts mean more red herrings, which is exactly what makes a Halloween mystery feel bigger than a typical dinner party game.

2. Let costumes do double duty

Since it's Halloween, you can skip the awkward "here's your costume" ask — tell guests their character dossier and a loose Halloween costume idea at the same time. A "small-town shopkeeper" or "suspicious newcomer" costume is an easy lift, and guests showing up already dressed as their character does more for the atmosphere than any decorations will.

3. Pace the evidence across three rounds

Haunted Hollow's evidence unfolds in three rounds rather than all at once — reveal one round between courses or candy-bowl breaks so the mystery has room to breathe. Dim the lights, light a few candles, and let guests debate theories between rounds instead of rushing straight to a verdict.

4. Save the host's solution for the final reveal

Every kit includes a host-only solution script, written to be read aloud once final guesses are locked in. Halloween adds one extra trick: read it by candlelight, or have the host step just outside the circle of light for the confession — it's a small touch that makes the reveal land harder.
