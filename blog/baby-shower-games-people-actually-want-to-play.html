5 Baby Shower Games People Actually Want to Play

no awkward icebreakers required

4-minute read · Updated November 2026

Most baby shower games fall into one of two camps: the kind everyone quietly dreads (measuring the mom-to-be's belly with yarn) or the kind that gets a room of mixed-generation, mixed-energy guests laughing together. Here are five in the second camp. They're gender-neutral, easy to print, and built to fill the gaps between opening gifts without dragging.

1. Baby Bingo, played during gift-opening

Give every guest a bingo card of likely shower gifts (onesies, diapers, a stuffed animal, anything monogrammed) before the gifts start. They mark off squares as the mom-to-be opens each present. It keeps everyone watching instead of scrolling their phone, and someone always yells "bingo" at exactly the right moment.

Why it works: gift-opening is the longest, quietest part of most showers. Bingo turns it into a game without adding a single minute to the schedule.

2. Guess the Baby Food, blind-tasted

Peel the labels off a handful of baby food jars, number them, and have guests guess the flavor by taste (or by smell, for the squeamish). It's a two-minute game with a guaranteed laugh, because nobody guesses "butternut squash" correctly on the first try.

What you need: five or six jars, small spoons, and an answer sheet for each guest.

3. A nursery-themed word scramble

A short list of baby-related words, scrambled into puzzles, doubles as a quiet activity for guests to work on before the main games start. It's especially useful if people trickle in over 15 to 20 minutes, since early arrivals have something to do while you wait.

Tip: set a stack of scrambles and pens at each table with a "first to finish wins" note, and the game runs itself.

4. Advice and wishes cards instead of a guest book

Skip the traditional guest book and hand out advice cards instead, with one line for parenting advice and one for a wish for the baby. They're lovely read aloud during a lull, and the parents keep the stack afterward instead of a book that gets shelved.

Grandparents tend to write the best ones. Expect at least one card that makes the room cry and one that makes it laugh.

5. Guess the Price, for the gift-savvy crowd

Display a few baby items and have guests guess the retail price. The closest guess without going over wins. It's light, low-prep, and a nice filler between bigger activities. It also tends to shock the guests who haven't shopped for baby gear in a few decades.

Games to skip (and why)

A few classics cause more cringing than laughing. Consider leaving these off the list:

  • Measure the belly. Few expecting parents enjoy a room guessing their waistline.
  • The melted candy bar diaper game. It gets a laugh from some crowds and a lot of grimaces from others. Know your audience.
  • Anything that puts one person on the spot for too long. Long solo quizzes about the parents can drag for guests who don't know them well.

The common thread with the five games above: none need props you don't already have, and every one keeps the whole room involved instead of putting the mom-to-be on the spot for twenty minutes straight.

How many games should you plan?

For a typical two-hour shower, three or four games is plenty. A rough schedule that works:

  • First 20 minutes: arrivals, food, and a self-running game like the word scramble.
  • Next 30 minutes: one or two active games, like Guess the Baby Food or Guess the Price.
  • Next 45 minutes: gift-opening with Baby Bingo running alongside.
  • Last 20 minutes: cake, advice cards read aloud, and goodbyes.

Leave room for conversation. The guests came to celebrate the parents, not to win a tournament.

Prizes that people actually want

Small, consumable prizes go over best: a fancy candle, a gourmet chocolate bar, a coffee shop gift card, or a nice hand soap. Skip anything that needs dusting. Have a few more prizes than games in case of ties.

Frequently asked questions

Are these games gender-neutral?

Yes. All five work for any baby and any crowd, including co-ed showers.

Can I play these at a virtual shower?

Most of them, yes. Email the printable to guests ahead of time, and Bingo, Word Scramble, Guess the Price, and advice cards all work over video.
